# Primary intraday evidence after 26 failed policies and hypotheses

2026-10-06. Literature and design review only. No historical return scan, fitting, signal computation, simulation or source/core change was performed. The authoritative inventory remains [0/26 historical hurdle passes](minute_strategy_results.md). This memo proposes one untested mechanism; it does not register candidate 27 or promise a successful example.

The genuinely distinct price-only candidate is **clock-driven return concentration at 15-minute candle boundaries**, rather than another price-trend threshold. A time-of-day momentum rule is worth understanding, but its directional input is still past return momentum and should not automatically be counted as a new independent economic edge. The earlier [mechanism memo](next_mechanism_research.md) correctly warns against mining calendar or episode definitions from this already examined year.

## Inspectable primary evidence

Shanaev, Vasenin and Stepanov, *Turn-of-the-candle effect in bitcoin returns*, Heliyon 9, e14236, available online **2 March 2023**, studies exchange-provided one-minute Bitcoin prices on seven exchanges, with differing start dates through December 2021. It reports concentration of positive returns at minutes 0, 15, 30 and 45, averaging roughly **0.58 basis points per boundary minute** in 2021. The proposed explanation is trading around completed 15-minute information. Its Bitfinex simulation holds Bitcoin during these boundary minutes; out-of-sample checks use January–August 2022. Its profitability claim depends on rolling-volume fee reductions, eventually reaching a zero-fee tier, and includes an estimated spread. This is not evidence for Binance perpetual fees, current quotes, altcoins, or our 2025–2026 year. The paper's return-label-to-execution timestamp convention must be checked before any replication; a statistical minute label alone does not prove an achievable fill. [Primary author-hosted full paper, sections 3–4 and tables 1/5](https://researchportal.northumbria.ac.uk/ws/portalfiles/portal/121797908/1_s2.0_S2405844023014433_main.pdf).

Gao, Han, Li and Zhou, *Market intraday momentum*, Journal of Financial Economics, **August 2018**, uses SPY transaction prices from **1 February 1993 through 31 December 2013**. The predictor includes the previous stock-market close through the first half-hour; it predicts the final half-hour. The market-timing trade goes long or short at the start of that last half-hour according to the signal. Hence the familiar stock-clock example is information through 10:00 New York, trade approximately 15:30–16:00: a **30-minute holding period**, not one minute. Crypto has neither that overnight closure nor a mandatory closing auction; selecting analogous UTC hours is an additional hypothesis. [Primary publisher methods](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0304405X18301351), [author institution publication record](https://profiles.wustl.edu/en/publications/market-intraday-momentum/).

Shen, Urquhart and Wang, *Bitcoin intraday time series momentum*, first published **26 October 2021**, Financial Review 57(2), May 2022, specifically acknowledges continuous Bitcoin trading. Its abstract describes volume-defined market sessions and positive first-to-last-half-hour predictability, stronger with initial-session volume or volatility, with a liquidity-provision interpretation. That supports investigating a clock-conditioned mechanism, not importing stock-session times unchanged. The accessible publisher page gives abstract/references; the accepted manuscript was discoverable but direct access returned an access-denied page during this review. Consequently its exact sample dates, selected session anchors, predictor construction, cost assumptions and trading clocks were **not independently verified here**. We should obtain and inspect those methods before claiming replication. [Primary publisher](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/fire.12290), [accepted manuscript location](https://centaur.reading.ac.uk/100181/3/21Sep2021Bitcoin%20Intraday%20Time-Series%20Momentum.R2.pdf).

Hansen, Kim and Kimbrough, *Periodicity in Cryptocurrency Volatility and Liquidity*, first arXiv submission **24 September 2021**, studies Bitcoin/Ether at Coinbase Pro, Binance and Uniswap V2, finding recurring volume and volatility patterns. This is supporting evidence for a periodic trading environment; neither volume periodicity nor volatility periodicity establishes a signed net-return premium. [Primary preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.12142).

These publications predate our fixed year. Their publication dates are not lookahead within the year, but our present selection of which literature to pursue is retrospective. A September 2026 session-strategy paper surfaced in searching and was not used to select this hypothesis: it postdates the sample start and openly uses full-sample session/rule selection.

## Cost and execution blockers

Keep the already disclosed signed-perpetual assumptions: taker fee 5 bps per side, adverse slippage 2 bps for BTC/ETH and 8 bps for DOGE/PEPE. First-order round trips therefore cost **14 bps BTC/ETH** and **26 bps DOGE/PEPE** per entry notional; joint doubled-fee/slippage stress costs **28/52 bps**. These are model assumptions, not verified historical quotes. Original spot assumptions differ: 10 bps fees plus 2 bps BTC slippage cost **24 bps** per round trip. A cross-venue four-fill pair is a different cost structure.

Comparing the paper's 0.58 bps average target-minute move with the unchanged 14 bps BTC perpetual round trip gives a gross/cost ratio of only about 0.041 before funding, adverse selection, capacity, tick rounding and partial cleanup. That arithmetic is counterevidence for this implementation; it is not a measurement of our year. We cannot adopt Bitfinex zero fees, manufacture maker fills, assume discounts from turnover, lengthen holdings or choose a subset of hours to rescue the effect without adding new hypotheses.

Known calendar times permit orders before the target interval, but minute OHLC cannot validate spread, queue or sub-minute latency. A fixed **one-minute intended holding period** is particularly vulnerable to a one-bar stressed delay: the stressed entry can reach the scheduled exit. A defensible implementation must skip that expired entry, or execute explicit cleanup if it has actually filled; it must never retroactively buy the start price or extend the trade using future returns. This distinguishes one-minute data (also suitable for 30-minute session trades) from a strategy that actually intends one-minute holdings.

## One fixed proposal, not an implementation or preregistration

If the economic-cost objection is nevertheless to be falsified under the existing account model, freeze this single specification before inspecting prices: long only during **[T,T+1 minute)** where UTC T has minute 0/15/30/45, cash otherwise; all seven weekdays, no preferred hours, no reversal version, no threshold or holding-period grid. The direction is calendar-only, not a function of the preceding return. Explicitly label this open-to-next-open interval convention as our executable adaptation, pending confirmation of the paper's return indexing. Do not count all eight coins as eight mechanisms.

Use the existing eight native Binance perpetual instruments, with 1000PEPE units retained, eight segregated $12,500 sleeves, leverage one and fixed 25% entry notional. No outside-year warmup, transfers or interval rebalancing. Unfilled or partially filled entry/exit orders obey existing capacity and cleanup rules; unresolved inventories remain marked and block overlapping entries. Funding is actual cashflow if exposure exists at settlement, independent of predictor assumptions. Avoid future final-quarter prices when sizing or determining terminal state.

The first six months (2025-10-05–2026-04-05) may serve only a frozen economic-admissibility check, not select clocks: the one-sided seven-day-block lower confidence bound with total 1% error budget split equally across the eight assets (0.01/8 per asset) on each asset's daily-averaged target-interval return must exceed that asset's fixed stressed round-trip cost. Otherwise that sleeve stays cash for all subsequent phases. No tuning after an admission failure. Freeze 10,000 circular seven-day blocks and seed 42; retain no-admission results. This deliberately stringent screen would need explicit preregistration before any computation; there is currently no fitted result. Three fresh-wallet validation months precede the reused July–October evaluation and joint stress; disclose every idle and losing sleeve. Preserve prior economic gates, cost stresses and complete minute drawdown/ledger audits. Any actual trial would increment the disclosed family count to 27, with corresponding cumulative comparison accounting; bootstrap intervals remain conditional on an adaptively reused year.

Minimal pre-execution tests should cover calendar membership/prefix invariance, price future mutation, no target-close sizing, UTC and end boundaries, order-clock expiry under a one-bar delay, genuine partial cleanup, native PEPE quantities, same-clock funding, and a no-admission/all-cash outcome. The above is a proposal for review, not authorization to fit or run it.

**Recommendation:** do not present this paper as a likely profitable Binance strategy. The distinct mechanism exists in primary literature, but the most direct implementation has an adverse ex-ante cost comparison. Session momentum is a better candidate for longer holdings only after its complete primary methods are available and a scientifically justified clock is frozen; it is not yet an independently specified new edge. None of this resolves the absence of an untouched future validation sample or supplies four or five robust successful strategies.
