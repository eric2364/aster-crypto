# Cross-sectional perpetual funding: coverage assessment

**The 26 disclosed policies have not tested long-low-funding / short-high-funding perpetuals across different underlyings in one jointly simulated wallet.** That is a meaningful untested portfolio hypothesis, although it shares funding persistence and crowding information with previous policies. It would be one additional economically related hypothesis, not evidence of an independent edge or a successful example. This review inspected frozen definitions and source only; it did not fit parameters, calculate new historical signals or inspect new returns. The existing **0/26** result is unchanged.

## What the research does and does not support

Bryan Vine's [author-published experiment](https://bryanvine.github.io/alpha-research/paper2.html) explicitly separates same-asset cash-and-carry from a dollar-balanced basket buying low-funding and selling high-funding perpetuals. It reports results on a different 30-name, multi-year panel. This is a personal research report, not peer-reviewed validation or our reproduced winner. Its stated implementation uses spot returns as a perpetual-price proxy and lacks separate historical perpetual marks/order books and a liquidation-tail model. Those limitations matter for a real collateral account. The reported basket drawdown and modest out-of-sample Sharpe would not automatically meet our own hurdles. We should borrow the economic distinction, not its claimed success, parameter search or selected lookback.

[Koijen, Moskowitz, Pedersen and Vrugt's Carry](https://www.nber.org/papers/w19325) distinguishes carry from expected price appreciation across several asset classes; it provides conceptual precedent rather than minute-altcoin evidence. [Schmeling, Schrimpf and Todorov's Crypto Carry](https://www.bis.org/publications/working-paper-1087-crypto-carry) studies futures/spot carry and capital/margin frictions in BTC/ETH. Its cross-exchange evidence does not itself validate this eight-asset cross-sectional perpetual strategy.

## Required economic and execution distinctions

For signed native quantity `q_i`, an actual funding settlement contributes `−q_i × sourced_settlement_mark_i × rate_i`; price P&L, trading fees and liquidation charges remain separate. Low funding can still be positive, so its long pays funding; high funding can be negative, so its short pays. A positive **predicted difference**, not merely the existence of ranks, is the proposed carry source. The predictor must use only already published settlements with explicit lag, comparable calendar-duration treatment, stale suppression and ambiguous-cadence handling. Actual payments occur independently at each sourced settlement clock. Do not accrue a known historical future payment retroactively, compare raw rates across different settlement intervals, or add funding to equity twice.

Dollar-balanced entry notionals do **not** hedge different asset prices exactly. Basket beta, sector/meme exposure, dispersion, squeezes and notional drift remain. Calling the basket delta-neutral or market-neutral without qualification would be misleading. A separate trained price-beta hedge would introduce estimation, extra transactions and another defined exposure policy; it cannot be silently added after losses. An economic packet must decide beforehand whether it targets dollar balance only or an explicit causal factor hedge, and report the residual exposures.

Use one actual Engine run containing all eight native Binance perpetuals and one funded USDT wallet. The existing [Portfolio](../aster/backtest/portfolio.py) supports same-venue multiple instruments; margin mode must be explicitly fixed. Isolated positions retain local liquidation risk despite opposite basket gains; cross margin pools derivative P&L within the venue but still reserves gross initial margin and maintenance. Long/short notionals must never be netted to zero collateral or treated as short-sale cash proceeds. The native PEPE instrument remains `1000PEPEUSDT` without a spot conversion.

The coordinator needs bounded aggregate gross exposure, joint admission after all intended orders/costs, fixed or explicitly scheduled quantities, immutable pending GTC targets, non-atomic fill monitoring, failed-leg cancellation/cleanup and whole-account risk aborts. Adverse synthetic tests should cover one losing/illiquid leg, rank changes with orders pending, asymmetric funding clocks, shared-wallet margin denial, liquidation despite balanced entry and terminal marked residuals. Account returns must include all legs and funding/costs; profitable funding alone is not success. Basket breadth should measure actual participation and disclose every leg's loss rather than demand every hedge leg be profitable.

## Next decision

A pre-outcome **one-family joint funding-rank packet** is scientifically meaningful if the goal permits another exploratory comparison on the reused fixed year. Existing native perpetual bars, marks and settled funding supply its principal inputs; new OI data is not required for this mechanism. The packet still needs prospective economic defaults, turnover/holding and cost budget, collateral policy, execution tests and independent review before any new historical signals or outcomes. No particular window, threshold, weighting scheme, hedge fit or hurdle change is authorized by this memo. Retain all prior failures and increase the disclosed comparison count only when a new frozen experiment is actually undertaken. Neither external author results nor different portfolio construction can establish prospective robustness on a year already examined.
