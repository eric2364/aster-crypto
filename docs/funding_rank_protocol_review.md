# Independent prospective funding-rank protocol review

2026-10-06. Review of the draft [candidate 27 protocol](../examples/funding_rank/proposed_protocol.md), before feature construction, fitting or historical economic runs. No frozen earlier source, input, policy or outcome is changed. The existing 0/26 result remains authoritative; this is not a tested or fully preregistered candidate yet.

The proposal is a different joint portfolio specification: rank eight different Binance perpetual underlyings by lagged, duration-weighted settled funding; buy two low-rate contracts and sell two high-rate contracts in one cross-margin account. This has not been implemented by the earlier equal-underlying two-venue funding pair or single-asset crowding/fade studies. Its economic intuition is nevertheless related to previous funding contrarian hypotheses. It should count as one additional candidate, not four hedged edges, and it does not establish an independent mechanism merely by changing the portfolio construction.

The initial equal-dollar long and short totals are balanced; exposure is not beta-neutral or riskless. Funding persists only if future realized payments resemble the known history, and price-factor, basis, jump and liquidity risk can dominate carry. Lot rounding, different fill prices and later mark changes also alter dollar weights. A positive unweighted gap between the two highest and two lowest ranks mostly follows from sorting when rates are not all equal. It does not establish a positive retained-notional funding forecast or compensation for fees. The latter forecast can change sign through actual weights and marks. Keeping it as a disclosed diagnostic, rather than retroactively adding a profit-selecting cost threshold, is coherent for a fixed risk-bearing cross-sectional hypothesis.

The proposed causal predictor reuses the unchanged audited known-subset funding semantics: original receipt when supplied plus processing lag, otherwise an explicitly assumed settlement-plus-minute clock; strict availability before the decision; predecessors rebuilt only from already known settlements; actual recognized elapsed durations and seven-day overlap weights; conservative mixed-cadence, invalid-interval, coverage and stale-data rejection. All eight inputs must be valid for a proposal. Invalid held inputs close the retained four-leg basket; invalid unused inputs do not change an existing basket. No pre-year predecessor or future completed rate is permitted. This still cannot prove original historical publication times or detect a systematically missing cadence that resembles another valid cadence.

The fixed weekly selection and unchanged ranks throughout the pending roll are defensible causal rules. Closing all actual inventory and resolving tracked orders before sizing new targets avoids an atomic-flip fiction. The new quantities must use the fresh known account balance and completed prices at that later sizing clock, not the old basket's quantity or future entry fills. Per-leg native quantities remain fixed after sizing. Subminimum dust, a failed fourth leg and unresolved GTC orders must block a new basket rather than become a reporting-only warning. Normal roll and daily signal closure need no failure cooldown; failure/risk cleanup starts its 24-hour clock only when the real account is flat, and actual liquidation must latch failure even if core forced fills already flattened everything.

## Fixed validation and required source tests

They do not become historically exact exchange rules. Initial dollar balance must be reported alongside actual long/short notional drift and all eight native leg contributions; no profitable-leg filtering or synthetic funding-only equity is acceptable.

Five fixed fresh-wallet phases and the 15 specifically listed gates fit this portfolio structure. They should be kept literally frozen rather than copying neighbor or alternative-mark gates from another model. All losing legs, inactive folds, aborted baskets and residuals stay visible.

Independent causal tests should exercise future records with past settlement but unavailable receipt; delayed older receipts changing a predecessor only after actual availability; same-clock strict lag; asynchronous millisecond settlements; equal-rate fixed-order ties; full eight-valid refusal; daily retained-identity checks; no outside-year warmup; native quantity/price units; and future price mutation around Sunday and roll completion. The common year has already been examined through 26 policies. A frozen additional test can falsify this specific hypothesis, but cannot restore untouched holdout status or certify non-overfitting.
